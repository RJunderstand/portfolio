I build AI-assisted tools for the slow parts of university work, and I handle the people side of the same work: stakeholder management, student engagement, and presentations that change with the audience, from doctoral researchers to professional services teams. Everything on this page shipped in a real job.

How I work

It starts with observation: friction in my own workflow, a colleague's recurring pain point, a process students quietly struggle with. Nothing gets built unless it serves a defined learning or service outcome.

Before designing, I establish what the solution must comply with: the Equality Act 2010, WCAG 2.2 AA, UK GDPR and institutional policy. Compliance goes in at the start rather than getting patched on at the end.

Generative AI provides structure and speed, but a person keeps agency over the process. I review every decision, output and edge case, and test with the people who will actually use the tool. Each failure is data: reflect, adjust, retest until it holds up.

Delivery isn't the end. I keep optimising in live use, document the process, and make the model replicable so colleagues can adopt it for their own problems. Where possible I release it as open source, so it helps people beyond one campus.

Why I turned an emotion-recognition project into its opposite

Gentle Mirror began as an experiment in reading emotions from faces. I rebuilt it as the reverse: a browser-based piece where facial expressions, rather than inferred emotions, gently shift the weather on screen. Fully on-device (MediaPipe), nothing recorded, nothing transmitted. More importantly, it sits on the wrong side of what I think technology should ask of people. Built in Manchester, as a small argument against having to perform "fine" to be OK.
